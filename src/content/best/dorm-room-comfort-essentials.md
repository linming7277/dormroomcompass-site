## The picks are not substitutes for one another

This shortlist is organized by problem rather than an overall score. The AROEVE MK06 is the filtration pick, the Koonie is the airflow pick, and the MyHalos mask is the light-control pick. Calling one an overall winner would hide the most important decision: what is making the room uncomfortable?

## Why the AROEVE MK06 leads the shortlist

The MK06 has the clearest small-space appliance case in the group. AROEVE lists a 6.1 by 6.1 by 9.6-inch body at 3 pounds, a sleep-mode claim, a filter reminder, and a replacement interval of three to six months. Those details make placement and upkeep easier to estimate before buying.

Its limitation is evidence depth. EPA guidance says room size and CADR matter, and our collected sources do not include an independently verified CADR for the MK06. Choose it for a compact filtration format with understood maintenance—not because a room-area claim guarantees a result in every dorm.

## Why the Koonie is the airflow pick

The exact Koonie listing describes an 8-inch rechargeable fan with clip-and-desk placement, three speeds, two spray modes, a 200 mL tank, and a 10,000 mAh battery. The flexible placement is the useful dorm feature: it may put airflow near a lofted bed or crowded study area without depending on a nearby outlet during use.

Its tradeoffs are charging, water care, and limited scope. Runtime and airflow were not independently tested. Mist adds a tank to fill and clean, and it may be unsuitable around electronics or bedding. Choose it for personal airflow; do not expect whole-room cooling.

## Why MyHalos is the light-control pick

The MyHalos mask addresses a problem the powered products cannot: unwanted light. The official page describes contoured eye cups, an adjustable strap, and hand-wash care. It is compact, portable, and independent of outlet or housing-appliance rules.

Its value is highly personal. Strap comfort, face shape, side-sleeping posture, and light leakage vary. It cannot fix noise, heat, or a poor schedule. Choose it only when ambient light is the problem you can name.

## Choose none when the room needs a different fix

Skip all three when the issue is a building ventilation, temperature, moisture, or electrical problem that belongs with residence staff. Skip them when placement would block airflow, create a spill risk, or intrude on a roommate's space. Also skip them when existing blinds, furniture changes, or shared equipment already solve the problem.

The best dorm essential is sometimes the one you do not have to store, clean, charge, or replace.
